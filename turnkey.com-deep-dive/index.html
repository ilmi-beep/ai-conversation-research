In five lines

For the leadership team

  1. AI answers. Google showed an AI Overview for 10 of our 10 searches and cited turnkey.com in 2. ChatGPT answered all 10 and cited or named Turnkey in 4.
  2. Reach. Your robots.txt lets in every crawler we checked, and in our test your home page answered requests that announced themselves as AI crawlers.
  3. Read. 346 of 346 pages carry structured data, all of it in the HTML your server sends, and 770 of 772 code blocks parse. We found 4 things to fix in it.
  4. Read is the gap. Broken structured data on 2 pages is ignored by search engines and AI. 149 pages describe the business or a job title as a person. Your structured data links to 38 IDs that no page defines. More are listed below.
  5. Next. 7 fixes: 4 high, 2 medium, 1 low. 4 take a day or less. Start with "the Person markup", then "the broken ID links".

What to fix first

The biggest problems, in the order an AI tool meets them. A tool that cannot reach or read your pages never gets as far as trusting them.

149 pages describe the business or a job title as a person

Failed check: Each Person in your structured data holds one person's name only

▲ High Gate 2: read Error

Why it matters. A Person named after the business, or with a job title inside the name, makes machines think there is a person by that name. Facts about your business and your people get mixed up.

Fix. Use Organization for the business. For people, put only the name in name and the job title in jobTitle.

Your structured data links to 38 IDs that no page defines

Failed check: Every link inside your structured data points to something that is defined on your site

■ Medium Gate 2: read Error

Why it matters. An @id (an ID inside the code) lets one block say, for example, "this article's publisher is our business". If the ID it points to is not defined anywhere, the link leads nowhere, so machines cannot tell who published the page.

Fix. Point these links at an ID that is defined, usually the business's main ID on the home page, or define the missing ID once.

6 pages are marked up as the wrong kind of page

Failed check: Each page is marked up as the kind of thing it shows: an article as an article, a product as a product, an event as an event

▲ High Gate 2: read Error

Why it matters. The type in the structured data tells search engines and AI what the page is. An article marked up as a whole blog, or a product page with no product, is filed under the wrong kind of thing and misses the features for its real kind.

Fix. In each page template, use the type that fits the page: BlogPosting or Article for a single article or case study, Product for a product, Event for an event. Use Blog only on the blog's index page.

4 more findings (2 high, 1 medium, 1 low), also listed under each gate below

Broken structured data on 2 pages is ignored by search engines and AI

Failed check: Every block of structured data (code that tells search engines and AI what a page is about) is valid code

▲ High Gate 2: read Error

Why it matters. A block of structured data with a single syntax error is thrown away whole. Search engines and AI tools read nothing from it.

Fix. Fix the code in the template that writes these blocks, then test a page in the Schema Markup Validator (validator.schema.org).

Done when: Every block of structured data on these pages passes the Schema Markup Validator.

Effort: A day or less

Your structured data leaves out much of what your pages talk about

Failed check: Your structured data declares the business, products and services your pages talk about

▲ High Gate 3: quote Error

Why it matters. AI tools quote facts they can tie to a clear thing: this product, made by this business. If your pages mention products and services that the code never declares, AI has to guess what they are and who offers them.

Fix. Declare each product and service a page is about (Product, Service) and link it to the business (brand, provider or offers).

What we found, and how you know it is done
  • Products: mentioned on 8 of the 8 sampled pages, declared on 0.
  • Services: mentioned on 8 of the 8 sampled pages, declared on 0.

Examples

  • services: mentioned on 8 of the 8 sampled pages, declared on 0

Done when: On the sampled pages, the structured data declares the business and at least half of the products and services the text mentions.

Effort: Longer work

Your markup gives the business 5 names and the website 1 name

Failed check: Your structured data uses one name for the business and one name for the website

● Low Gate 4: trust Error

Why it matters. Search engines and AI tools treat different names as possibly different businesses or sites. Facts, reviews and mentions get split between them instead of adding up.

Fix. Use "Turnkey" as the business name in every Organization block (the code that names the business). Describe the site with one WebSite block (the code that names the website) for the whole site, named "Turnkey", instead of a separate WebSite for each section such as the blog.

What we found, and how you know it is done
  • Articles and case studies: 1 of 234

Examples

  • https://www.turnkey.com/: "Turnkey" is used as the business name (Organization) on 346 pages
  • https://www.turnkey.com/blog/building-internal-ai-agents: "Ramp" is used as the business name (Organization) on 1 page
  • https://www.turnkey.com/blog/building-internal-ai-agents: "Stripe" is used as the business name (Organization) on 1 page
  • https://www.turnkey.com/blog/building-internal-ai-agents: "Shopify" is used as the business name (Organization) on 1 page

Done when: Every Organization block names the business "Turnkey", and every page uses the same single WebSite block.

Effort: A day or less

Wikidata does not fully describe your business and its group

Failed check: The business has a Wikidata item that records its group

■ Medium Gate 4: trust Opportunity

Fix. Create or update the Wikidata items for the business and its group, with the official website and ownership, citing public sources. WLDM confirms the right items with you first.
